# Where jobs are posted

Most jobs reach you through two kinds of places:

- **The employer's own careers page.** This is the real posting. It is run on a "hiring platform" (also called an applicant tracking system, or ATS) such as Workday or Greenhouse.
- **Job boards** such as LinkedIn, Indeed or Job Bank. These copy or link to postings from many employers. They are good for discovering jobs, but they are not the source.

This page explains what to expect on each one, so you know when you need an account, how to spot a duplicate, and where to look.

## Five rules that apply everywhere

1. **Open the employer's own posting before you trust a board listing.** Boards can show postings that have closed, have been edited, or were copied incorrectly. Click through to the employer's careers page and check the title, location, salary and closing date there. If you cannot find the posting on the employer's own site, treat it as possibly closed. (Exception: on Job Bank, postings marked "Posted on Job Bank" were created there by the employer; follow their "How to apply" instructions even if the employer's website has no copy.)
2. **The requisition number identifies a posting.** Most postings show a "Job ID", "Req ID" or "Requisition #" (for example `R-123456` or `JR0098765`). Write it down when you apply. A new number usually means a new opening, but employers sometimes repost the same role under a new number. Before applying again, compare the employer, title, team and location with what you already applied to.
3. **The same job can have many links.** Board links often carry tracking codes (things after a `?` such as `?source=linkedin` or `utm_campaign=...`), or the employer lists one job in several cities. Different links to the same requisition are **not** new jobs. Do not apply twice; check the requisition number instead.
4. **Save the posting when you apply.** Postings disappear when they close. Open the employer's posting, use your browser's **Print** command and choose **Save as PDF**, and keep the PDF with your application so you can prepare for an interview later.
5. **Never pay to apply, and never send banking or ID details before a real offer.** Legitimate employers do not charge application fees.

## Hiring platforms (on the employer's careers page)

### Workday

- Web addresses look like `company.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/...`.
- **Most employers' Workday sites ask you to create a candidate account, and each employer's account is separate.** Your account at one bank does not work at another bank's Workday site. Follow the sign-in options shown on that employer's application page, and keep a list of which employers you have accounts with (a password manager helps).
- Workday usually asks you to upload a resume and then re-type parts of it into forms. Check what it filled in: the automatic reading is often wrong.
- You can return later to see your application status under "Candidate Home".
- Large Workday sites sometimes refuse automated tools and ask you to try again. If a page will not load, wait a minute and reload in a normal browser.
- Job alerts: after signing in, use "Create job alert" on a search to be emailed new matches.

### Greenhouse

- Addresses look like `job-boards.greenhouse.io/company` or `boards.greenhouse.io/company`, or a form embedded on the company site.
- No account needed. Each posting has its own form; you fill it in again for every application.
- Many startups and software companies use it. The board page lists the jobs the employer publishes on that board, which is an easy way to browse them. Large employers sometimes keep separate boards for different brands.

### Lever

- Addresses look like `jobs.lever.co/company`.
- No account needed. One form per posting, usually short: resume, contact details and a few questions.

### Ashby

- Addresses look like `jobs.ashbyhq.com/company`.
- No account needed. One form per posting. The board page lists the jobs published on that board, often with filters by team and location.

### SmartRecruiters

- Addresses look like `jobs.smartrecruiters.com/Company` or `careers.smartrecruiters.com/Company`.
- You can apply as a guest. Creating a SmartRecruiters account lets you reuse your profile across employers that use it.

### iCIMS

- Addresses often contain `icims.com` (for example `careers-company.icims.com`).
- Usually asks you to create an account per employer, similar to Workday. Expect a resume upload followed by form pages.

### Taleo (Oracle Taleo)

- Addresses contain `taleo.net`. Common at older large employers, governments and hospitals.
- Choose **Apply as Guest** if the employer offers it; otherwise create an account for that employer. Forms can be long and time out; save your progress often.

### SAP SuccessFactors

- Addresses often contain `successfactors` (for example `career5.successfactors.com`), though many companies show it under their own web address.
- Usually asks you to create an account for each employer. Common at large international companies, banks, consulting firms and some universities.

### Oracle Recruiting (Oracle Cloud)

- Addresses contain `oraclecloud.com/hcmUI/CandidateExperience`.
- Usually no password: you type your email and receive a one-time code each time you sign in. Check your spam folder if the code does not arrive.

### Other systems

You will also meet Workable, Breezy HR, JazzHR, BambooHR, Jobvite, Pinpoint, Rippling, UKG and others. Most work like Greenhouse (one form per job, no account), but some ask you to create an account. The first page of the application tells you which.

### "Company site"

Some employers run their own careers page. When you click Apply, they often hand you off to one of the systems above, so the advice for that system then applies. Small companies may only list jobs on their website or on LinkedIn, or ask you to email a resume.

## Job boards

### LinkedIn

- Good for discovery and for seeing who works at an employer.
- **"Easy Apply"** sends your LinkedIn profile and a resume through LinkedIn. It is quick, but some employers pay less attention to it. If the posting has an "Apply on company website" button, prefer that.
- Many listings are reposts from recruiters or aggregators. Check that the employer and requisition number match the employer's own site.
- Job alerts: search, set filters (location, date posted, experience level), then turn on "Set alert". Use "Past 24 hours" or "Past week" to keep results fresh.
- "Promoted" listings are paid ads and appear at the top regardless of fit.

### Indeed

- Very broad, including smaller employers that use no other board.
- "Apply with Indeed" works like Easy Apply. Indeed also scrapes postings from employer sites, so some listings are copies; open the original.
- Job alerts: run a search and choose to get new jobs for it by email.

### Job Bank (Canada)

- The Government of Canada's free job board: `jobbank.gc.ca`.
- Every posting shows the employer and how to apply (often by email or the employer's site). Postings marked "Posted on Job Bank" were created there by the employer, so Job Bank can be the original source. Some postings are tied to work-permit processes; read the requirements carefully.
- Free "Job Match" and email alerts are available with a free account.

### University and hospital career sites

- Universities and hospitals usually post on **their own** site and do not always use LinkedIn or Indeed. Check them directly.
- Universities often have **separate sites for staff jobs and for faculty/academic jobs**, and sometimes a third for postdoctoral positions. Look for links such as "Staff positions", "Faculty positions" and "Postdoctoral opportunities".
- Research jobs at hospitals may be listed under the hospital's research institute rather than the main hospital careers page.
- Unionized positions often show a posting number, a closing date and a pay band. Applications after the closing date are usually not accepted.

### CharityVillage (Canada)

- `charityvillage.com` is the main board for Canadian non-profits and charities: program, research, grants, fundraising and policy roles.
- Some postings require a cover letter addressed to a named person. Read the "How to apply" section; it often asks for a specific email subject line or a single PDF.

### Academic job boards

- **HigherEdJobs** (`higheredjobs.com`): mostly US colleges and universities; strong for staff, research and administrative roles.
- **University Affairs** (`universityaffairs.ca`): the main board for Canadian faculty and academic positions.
- Also useful: **Nature Careers**, **Science Careers**, **Times Higher Education (THE) Jobs**, **jobs.ac.uk** (UK), **Academic Positions** (Europe), and the job pages of scientific societies in your field.
- Academic postings often ask for extra documents (research statement, teaching statement, list of referees). Note the closing date and whether referees are contacted automatically.

## Job alerts and feeds

- **Set alerts in a few places, not everywhere.** A good starting set: one or two boards (LinkedIn and Indeed, or a sector board) plus alerts on the careers sites of your top employers.
- **Make searches narrow.** Use the title phrases from [Disciplines and job titles](Disciplines%20and%20job%20titles.md) in quotes, add a location, and limit to recent postings.
- **Feeds have caps.** Boards and alert emails only show a limited number of results (for example the first few pages, or the top 25 per email). If a search returns hundreds of results, you are not seeing all of them; split it into narrower searches by title or location.
- **Alerts are a starting point.** Some employers never send their jobs to boards. Visit your top employers' careers pages directly every week or two.
- **Expect duplicates.** The same job can appear in several alerts. Match on employer and requisition number before adding it to your list.

## Quick checklist before you apply

- [ ] I opened the posting on the employer's own careers page.
- [ ] I noted the job ID (requisition number) and closing date, if shown.
- [ ] I checked that I have not already applied to this requisition.
- [ ] I saved a copy of the posting.
- [ ] I know whether this platform needs an account, and I have saved the login safely.
